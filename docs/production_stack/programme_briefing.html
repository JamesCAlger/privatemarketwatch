02 / What was completed

The design now has working evidence behind it.

A reproducible starting point

A separate copy of the pipeline rebuilt all 780,425 legacy holdings rows from frozen cached inputs. Its CSV exactly reproduced the earlier measurement input. Matching and PIK outputs were then regenerated from that same candidate.

The required BDC cohort and history account for 261,613 of those rows. Reproducing data reliably establishes a baseline; financial accuracy still needs source checks.

Concrete conventions and examples

The brief now covers source units, currency conversion and durable identities. It includes real filing examples for missing values, zero values, entity relationships, position continuity and different forms of PIK evidence.

Were the FX rules tested on real data?

Real filings were inspected for currency meaning and tagging errors. For example, one printed GBP principal amount had an EGP tag. Foreign principal can also sit beside cost and fair value reported in USD, so each field needs its own currency decision.

The conversion code used synthetic exchange rates. Its arithmetic and date-selection tests cover weekends, no future-rate use, signs, zero, stale/missing rates and ambiguous observations. We have not yet tested a complete path from a real filing amount through real historical rates to its USD equivalent.

The next proof is to save a dated historical-rate dataset, identify its exact version, and check real currency-resolved examples against independently calculated conversions. Versioning lets us reproduce the same result even if the provider later revises a quote.

A working route from a number to its evidence

The offline prototype opens a filing at the highlighted source cell. Calculated numbers link to their inputs; inferred values expose the reasoning and its limitations.

Open the citation prototype →

The current prototype uses HTML filings. PDF navigation remains a later option.

See the nine preparation items and their boundaries
  • Units and FX: document-level unit inventory, real tagging conflicts, fixed conversion/date/fallback rules and tested reference code. Real FX observations still need to be imported and versioned.
  • Source review: all 61 pilot packets have explicit outcomes. Four complete source holding lists were established for development; remaining evidence gaps are recorded.
  • Reference examples: twenty proposed corrections to legacy labels, eight boundary examples and four native PIK facts. Original labels remain intact.
  • Execution and recovery: frozen inputs, completed-stage records, timeout handling, integrity checks and a real restored copy. Production isolation still needs stronger enforcement.
  • Rules and measurements: a synchronized candidate and full accounting for the 22 applied B2 correction rules. Other rule families need broader interaction checks.
  • Durable identities: allocation, split, merge and amendment behavior tested, including a real Esdec example. Production IDs have not been migrated.
  • Evidence navigation: six real highlights across four filings, plus calculation, inference, missing-evidence and rejected-link paths.
  • Acceptance: numerical working targets and executable checks that retain failed and unassessable outcomes. Targets can be amended with evidence and a recorded version; the complete release gate remains to be implemented.
  • Execution controls: a proposed eight-hour tranche with job limits, recovery steps and escalation rules.
What was actually tested?

26 focused tests passed. The isolated rebuild, identity and PIK stages, real restore and browser checks also passed. The successful holdings build took 35.8 minutes; identity processing took about 9.5 minutes.

A completed 2.18GB result bundle was copied and verified in 6.52 seconds. This proves recovery at completed stages. Work interrupted inside a SQL statement must restart from its stage boundary.

All 36 measured source navigations passed. The local 95th-percentile time was 3.91 seconds. Keyboard access, mobile layout and evidence paths passed; no external browser requests occurred.

The full test suite and production frontend build were not run. The complete baseline file scan exceeded its four-minute limit; the narrower semantic comparison completed with the previously existing differences. This was not a clean production-baseline pass.

The execution prototype runs trusted copied code with checks. It is not yet a complete operating-system security boundary.

03 / What we learned

The remaining problems are now more specific.

Source coverage

Some disclosed holdings never reach the dataset.

There are 812 eligible fund-quarter records in the required history. Holdings are present for 779; the remaining 33 need explanation or remediation. A missing output alone cannot tell us whether the fund held no investments.

Three concrete missing-schedule examples

26North, December 2023: four disclosed positions with $52.566m signed fair value; the candidate has no rows.

Golub Private Credit, June 2023: a named money-market holding worth $650.323m; the candidate has no rows. A schedule containing only that instrument is still a holdings schedule.

Lord Abbett, September 2024: 33 disclosed positions and a $313.562m primary portfolio total; the candidate has no rows. Six fair-value dashes remain unresolved and cannot simply be treated as zero.

These examples refer to different quarters. Their amounts cannot be added into one current-quarter portfolio error rate.

Industry enrichment

Sector coverage is below the working target.

This is primarily an enrichment issue for holdings already captured. A missing code may need a classification, a corrected entity/cache match or a valid taxonomy mapping. It does not automatically require a new LLM inference.

Position and entity matching

Existing examples still reveal incorrect links.

The entity review also found 13 false merges and two false splits. These provide specific cases for engineering work.

The examples were already used during development. These counts are regression findings, not an unbiased estimate of overall matching accuracy. Complete position histories remain unmeasured.

Why some old reference labels also need correction

Twenty source-backed proposals distinguish candidate errors from reference errors. Aurelia had a EUR reference compared with a USD output. Sentinel used a joint-venture delayed-draw value for a parent term loan. Fifth Season's prior-period fair value was mistaken for current cost.

Some broad equity labels also lacked evidence for a precise common/preferred classification. These proposals are stored separately for review; they have not overwritten the original benchmarks.

What “portfolio-wide rule impact” now means

The full recorded population affected by 22 B2 correction applications has now been measured across cached dates and vehicles. Together they changed signed fair value by a net +$21.298m.

Adding the absolute fair-value changes separately for each fund-quarter gives $78.482m. This avoids positive and negative changes cancelling each other out.

One overlapping pair of rule footprints was observed. Reversing the B2 application order produced no differences in the tracked values for these inputs. This does not establish the effect, safety or correctness of every global and filer-specific rule in the pipeline.

04 / What remains

Build the controls, complete the evidence, then repair.

1

Make the execution boundary production-ready.

Turn the preparation runner into a supported process that rejects mixed or stale files, checks every required output and resumes only from verified completed stages.

2

Complete independent reference coverage.

Four complete holding lists are available as development examples. Of the other 57 reviewed packets, 48 still lack a certified full list, eight lack a primary source and one is evidenced as pre-investment. Sector truth and complete position histories need their own references.

3

Implement the quality contract and prioritize fixes.

Every one of the 28 acceptance criteria needs a recorded result. Then address the missing schedules and matching cases against explicit source expectations. The broader schema, identity and dbt migrations follow their agreed dependencies.

Why this work matters: without an independent reference, a cleaner pipeline can reproduce the same mistake more reliably. The next tranche makes improvements measurable and failures visible.

05 / Proposed standards

What “good enough” would mean.

Working targets supported in the 10 September review · amendable with evidence

Use these as the starting quality targets. As real data reveals unsuitable definitions or assumptions, the metrics can be revised. Current results include both failures and measures that cannot yet be assessed.

For each amendment, explain the source evidence, preserve the old version and compare old/new results on the same candidate where meaningful. Material changes to acceptable error or scope come back to you; routine measurement corrections can proceed within the agreed work.

At least 99%

Value coverage and correctness

Capture at least 99% of independently established source value, and have at least 99% of published value supported by genuine in-scope holdings, per quarter.

At least 99.5%

Reported amount accuracy

Amounts should agree at the precision disclosed in the source. Missing, unsupported and inapplicable fields need separate treatment.

At most 0.25%

Quarterly reconciliation error

Add absolute errors against independent fund totals, so overstatements and understatements cannot cancel. Apply source rounding and accounting-scope rules.

At least 99.5%

Correct identity links

Target this precision separately for entity and position links, alongside at least 95% recall of true links and 95% complete source-supported chains.

See the remaining thresholds and safeguards
  • History: account for 100% of required fund-quarter obligations, including evidenced empty or unavailable states.
  • Holding lists: at least 95% row recall and precision per quarter. Per-fund floors: 95% for fair value and 90% for rows.
  • Other numeric fields and dates: at least 99% correct, with source precision and missingness retained.
  • Individual fund reconciliation: ordinarily no more than 1% error, with rounding and zero-total handling.
  • Evidence behind reconciliation: comparable independent primary totals for at least 95% of eligible funds and 99% of source-side fair value. Unknown denominators cannot pass.
  • GICS sectors: at least 95% independently checked accuracy; codes available for at least 95% of absolute fair value and 90% of rows.
  • Citations: every value has an evidence path or an explicit unavailable status; zero misleading links. Correct highlights cover at least 99% of reported-amount fair value and 95% of reported numerical observations. Calculations and inferences expose their supporting paths.
  • Local navigation: 95th-percentile citation time no more than five seconds on the declared workload. Public deployment needs separate measurement.

For sampled accuracy, use a one-sided 95% statistical confidence bound appropriate to the actual sampling design. A small perfect sample is insufficient to establish a 99.5% accuracy claim.

The earlier $614.9m excess and the proposed absolute reconciliation error are different measures. The latter adds each fund's error without netting and requires comparable independent source totals.

The next deliverable

A tested execution and measurement package, four source-backed remediation packets, one integrated candidate, an explained change report and a clear list of unresolved issues. Its data-quality verdict may still be fail or unassessable.

Limits on the proposed eight-hour batch

Run one heavy job at a time. Cap a unified build at 90 minutes, identity processing at 60 minutes and PIK processing at 15 minutes. Monitor memory and preserve completed checkpoints. Allow one diagnosed execution retry and two distinct mechanism attempts per unresolved case.

Reserve time for verification and handoff within the eight-hour total. Stop affected work on unexplained changes, ambiguous financial meaning, missing authority or failed validation. Source downloads, paid research and production adoption require their specific authority.

The runner does not yet measure or enforce an agent-token budget. Recovery is based on saved artifacts and verified stage boundaries, without assuming that a session survives a usage interruption.
